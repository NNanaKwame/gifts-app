# Shared traits and Scripture

The main assessment now contains 63 initial statements: three direct prompts for each of 17 areas, plus 12 shared prompts. Deeper reflection adds one direct prompt per area, reaching exactly 80. Optional service and character sections remain separate. Unselected original questions remain in the data bank for editorial review but are not served by the core forms.

These are editorial applications of Scripture, not biblical diagnostic criteria. Narrative examples do not establish the named gifts of their participants. General Christian practices are not exclusive evidence of particular gifts. The mappings are intentionally non-exhaustive; tongues has no shared contribution rather than a speculative connection.

## Strengthening others through words

Related areas: Prophecy, Encouragement, Teaching, Interpretation of Tongues.

[1 Corinthians 14:3–6, 26; Acts 15:32](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Corinthians%2014%3A3%E2%80%936%2C%2026%3B%20Acts%2015%3A32&version=NIV). Paul connects intelligible contributions with building up the church; Judas and Silas illustrate prophetic encouragement. Applying this shared purpose to these prompts is interpretive.

- People have told me that something I shared helped them continue following Christ during a difficult time.
- When I speak with believers who are discouraged, I help them find encouragement in God’s truth.

## Helping others understand and apply truth

Related areas: Wisdom, Knowledge, Teaching, Discerning of Spirits.

[Colossians 1:28; 1 Corinthians 12:8–10; 1 John 4:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Colossians%201%3A28%3B%201%20Corinthians%2012%3A8%E2%80%9310%3B%201%20John%204%3A1&version=NIV). Teaching with wisdom is explicit; connecting understanding and evaluation with knowledge and discernment is an editorial application, not an exhaustive definition of those gifts.

- I have helped someone understand how a biblical truth relates to a situation they faced.
- When someone brings a confusing spiritual claim, I help them examine its meaning in light of Scripture.

## Coordinating practical service

Related areas: Service / Helps, Administration, Leadership.

[Acts 6:1–7; Romans 12:7–8; 1 Corinthians 12:28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%206%3A1%E2%80%937%3B%20Romans%2012%3A7%E2%80%938%3B%201%20Corinthians%2012%3A28&version=NIV). Acts describes a response to overlooked needs through shared responsibility. These modern category connections are inferred from the narrative, not gift labels assigned by Luke.

- When a shared ministry task is getting overlooked, I help people agree on who will do what.
- I have helped a group put a practical plan into action so that people were better served.

## Meeting concrete needs

Related areas: Service / Helps, Giving, Mercy.

[Acts 9:36–39; Romans 12:7–8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%209%3A36%E2%80%9339%3B%20Romans%2012%3A7%E2%80%938&version=NIV). Tabitha’s practical care illustrates overlap in service, generosity and mercy; Acts does not name her particular gifts.

- When someone is struggling, I look for a practical way to help with what they need.
- I have made time or shared resources to help someone whose needs were being overlooked.

## Praying with people in difficult circumstances

Related areas: Faith, Healings, Miracles.

[Acts 4:29–30; James 5:14–16; 1 Corinthians 12:9–10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%204%3A29%E2%80%9330%3B%20James%205%3A14%E2%80%9316%3B%201%20Corinthians%2012%3A9%E2%80%9310&version=NIV). These passages connect prayer, trust and requests for healing or signs. Prayer is for all believers and cannot establish a healing, miracle or a particular gift.

- When people face circumstances they cannot change, I join them in asking God for help.
- I have continued praying with someone through a difficult situation, trusting God while leaving the outcome to Him.

## Helping others begin and grow in service

Related areas: Pioneering gospel service (apostolic), Leadership, Teaching.

[Ephesians 4:11–13; Romans 12:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ephesians%204%3A11%E2%80%9313%3B%20Romans%2012%3A8&version=NIV). Equipping is a shared purpose of the ministries in Ephesians. Its application to pioneering service and Romans 12 leadership is interpretive and does not establish an office.

- I have helped another believer prepare to take responsibility for serving others.
- I help people take a first step in a ministry and support them as they learn.

## Scoring decision and limitations

For each gift, calculate the mean of its direct answers (reverse-score flagged items). Average each linked trait’s two responses, then average those trait means. This prevents multiple links from accumulating extra weight. If directMean > 3, add min(5 − directMean, max(0, sharedMean − 3) × 0.2); otherwise add zero. Rescale the resulting mean to 0–100. The maximum addition is 10 points. Missing direct or linked shared answers prevent that category from being scored. Unrelated optional answers never enter core scores.

The threshold, weight and mappings are design hypotheses, not numbers prescribed by Scripture. This supportive-only rule can change rankings and may favor mapped categories over tongues; that tradeoff requires review and pilot testing. Identical direct profiles remain inconclusive even if shared responses differ. Exact ties remain visible; small gaps are flagged. Scores describe answers, not the probability of possessing a gift. They do not authenticate prophecy, healing or miracles, or confer an office.

Love and general spiritual maturity never contribute points. The existing John 13:34 and 1 Corinthians 13 reminder remains beside results. Questionnaire version 2026-10-24.2 triggers the existing recovery/download flow for older sessions instead of silently rescoring them.

Before claiming improved accuracy, obtain church content review and a consent-based pilot comparing rankings with and without shared support, stability over time, clarity, completion burden and feedback from people who know participants’ service. More questions alone do not establish validity.
